I began solving the daily New York Times crossword puzzles in high school, and I knew right away that I wanted to construct and publish my own crosswords. Despite the fierce competition among puzzle submissions, with some estimates putting their acceptance rate at 3%, two years later I got my first puzzle into the NYT! I specialise in making wide-open themeless crosswords, the more difficult puzzles in US newspapers. Have a look!

Cherwell Variety Puzzles

In my second year at Oxford, I joined the student newspaper Cherwell as a puzzle writer and became the Puzzles Deputy Editor during Trinity Term. I started Cherwell's practice of variety word puzzles and logic puzzles, which often look like standard crosswords or sudoku but have a creative twist. During Trinity Term, I workshopped and formatted all puzzles on the page.

Below are some of my favourites from my time at Cherwell. All pages I worked on as writer and Deputy Editor can be seen here.

Explosive Low Ds

My favourite quiz podcast, Lateral with Tom Scott, used a question I sent in. Pretty exciting! Each question has an unusual premise, and guests inch towards the answer just with lateral thinking. This question is not strictly a "puzzle" like others on this page, but I designed it to be solvable without much prior knowledge, just like my other puzzles. If you have six minutes to spare, see if you can answer my question before the guests do!
