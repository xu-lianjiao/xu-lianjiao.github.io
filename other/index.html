Other Pursuits

I'm on the organising committee of the Oxford Mathematics Team Challenge (OMTC), a fully student-run national competition for sixth-form students across the UK. My classmate and I both come from the SF Bay and did the Berkeley and Stanford tournaments as kids, and we wanted to bring a similar spirit to the UK, where the demand for such an event was there but not the supply.

Thus we created OMTC in my second year, dedicated to sharing our love for high-level maths, open to any student for free. There aren't many opportunities for school students to spend a day doing hard but low-stakes maths problems in a place like Oxford's Examination Schools. As soon as we got all the logistics cleared, the reception from schools as soon as we began marketing was immediate. The students really love doing this!

Here are some pictures from the exciting day of! They don't reflect the half-year of work from the whole team of OMTC volunteers that made this one day happen. For the month leading up to the event, my friends and I would be spending more time on OMTC than on our degrees, so after it ends, we have two weeks of just locking in harder than ever. But every bit of that struggle is worth it!

I also spend a ton of time making music. I play classical piano in college recitals and soirées, funk/jazz keys in a combo that plays gigs in Oxford bar nights and parties, sing bass as a soloist in an English church choir, and lately I've been teaching myself bass and fingerstyle guitar. Compared to other technical skills, music is the one thing where I can stay out of the fast lane. It's nice to have an outlet, and I don't have to be a master of an instrument to consider myself a good musician. It's gotten me far enough to go on so many fun adventures, after all!

Connect Four

I made this bot after learning about minimax in Artificial Intelligence. This bot's thinking depth is set to 7 moves, with a heuristic function maximising its 4-in-a-row opportunities in the board. It's a tough bot, but it can be tricked!

MMabble

MMabble is 9x9 Scrabble where the only letter is M. As per the Scrabble dictionary, the only legal words in this game are MM and MMM. The first move must cover the star.

Watch for bonus squares, and note that playing overlaps is quite lucrative, since you get points for every word you form. Game ends at a stalemate, or when someone plays all off tiles on their rack.

Mumble

Mumble is 11x11 Scrabble with 40 Ms and 20 Us. As per the Collins Scrabble dictionary, there are ten legal words in this game: MM, MU, UM, MMM, MUM, UMM, UMU, MUMM, MUMU, and MUUMUU.

You can swap tiles with the bag if the bag has at least 7 tiles left. I wouldn't recommend it in this game though.
